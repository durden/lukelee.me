# Excellent Intermediate Python ebook
I was fortunate enough to serve as a Technical Editor on an excellent [Python](http://python.org) book written by [Matt Harrison](https://twitter.com/__mharrison__).  First off, you should check out the [book](http://www.amazon.com/Treading-Python-Volume-Intermediate-ebook/dp/B00BT95CWM/ref=sr_1_2?s=digital-text&ie=UTF8&qid=1364610301&sr=1-2) if you're a beginner or intermediate [Python](http://python.org) developer.

[Treading on Python Volume 2: Intermediate Python](http://www.amazon.com/Treading-Python-Volume-Intermediate-ebook/dp/B00BT95CWM/ref=sr_1_2?s=digital-text&ie=UTF8&qid=1364610301&sr=1-2) will push beginners outside of their comfort zone and provide a great review for intermediate and advanced developers on the functional components of the language.  I particularly enjoyed the tidbits in the book where Matt points out [Python 3](http://docs.python.org/3/whatsnew/3.0.html) differences.  So, if you haven't used the functional components of [Python 3](http://docs.python.org/3/whatsnew/3.0.html) much yet this is worth the price alone.

Also, if you enjoy the book Matt has tons of other great content to keep you busy.  Matt's [blog](http://hairysun.com/) has some good links on where to find some of his projects, etc.  He's also contributed some [great Pycon tutorials](https://speakerdeck.com/mattharrison/pycon-2013-tutorial-script-to-pypi-to-github-and-more).

He's entirely too prolific for me to really keep up!  So, if you're in the learning/reading mood here are a few more links to catch his teaching skills in action:
